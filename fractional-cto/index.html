Where I get called in

the four shapes of it

The roadmap outran the architecture

Shipping used to take days and now takes weeks. Nobody can point at the reason, and every fix looks like a rewrite.

The first senior hire

You need a technical bar more than you need headcount — someone who can tell a good engineer from a convincing one, and set the standard before it calcifies.

Due diligence is coming

An investor or an acquirer will read the code, the infrastructure and the delivery process. Better to know what they will find before they do.

Scale, suddenly

A customer win multiplies traffic, a regulator sets a date, or a migration cannot slip. This is the work I have done under load before.

How an engagement runs

four steps, no theatre
    1 · Diagnose (about two weeks)
    I read the code, the infrastructure and the delivery process, and talk to two or three engineers. You get an architecture map and a list of what actually carries risk.
    2 · Decide
    Two or three options with the trade-offs written down — cost in money and in weeks — so the decision outlives the meeting. Decision records are part of the deliverable.
    3 · Build, or lead the build
    I take the critical path myself, or guide your engineers through it and stay accountable for the outcome. Whichever leaves less mess behind.
    4 · Hand over
    Docs, runbooks and a team that can keep going without me. The goal of a fractional CTO is to stop being necessary.

Questions I get asked first

How much of your time does this take?
One to four days a month for a standing engagement — usually a weekly call plus a block of focused work. Reviews and due diligence are shorter: two to three weeks part-time, ending in a written deliverable.
How do you price it?
175 PLN per hour for advisory work. Retainers are quoted per month. Reviews and due diligence are fixed-scope: one price agreed before anything starts.
Do you replace an in-house CTO?
Either. Before the hire exists I hold the technical decisions and help you make the hire. With a CTO already in place I work as a counterpart for the hard calls, or on one specific problem.

What I don't do

  • No staff augmentation or outsourcing — I am not an agency, and I will not pretend to be a team.
  • No fixed-price rewrite of a system nobody has read yet.
  • No retainer without a decision to make. If there is nothing to decide, you do not need me monthly.
