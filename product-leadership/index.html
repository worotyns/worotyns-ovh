Where I get called in

the four shapes of it

Pricing that leaves money on the table

One price for everyone, a tier nobody buys, a discount culture nobody chose. The fix is usually structural, and it is uncomfortable before it is obvious.

A roadmap that is a wish list

Forty items, no ordering principle, and every customer request gets a ticket. What is missing is a decision about what not to build.

Positioning nobody can repeat

Your own team describes the product three different ways, and the website describes it a fourth. Buyers do not buy what they cannot retell.

The first product hire

You need product process before you need a Head of Product — a cadence, a discovery habit, and someone who can run it after I leave.

How an engagement runs

four steps, no theatre
    1 · Read the numbers
    Funnel, churn, price realisation, the roadmap you are actually shipping against, and the two or three customers who matter most. A week, mostly interviews.
    2 · Name the real problem
    Written down, in one page, with the evidence. Half of the value is watching a team agree on the diagnosis before anyone proposes a fix.
    3 · Change one thing at a time
    Pricing, positioning or roadmap — in that order of leverage. I run the change with your team rather than handing over a plan for it.
    4 · Leave a playbook
    How pricing is reviewed, how the roadmap gets prioritised, how discovery happens. Written for the team that stays, not for me.

Questions I get asked first

What does a pricing engagement produce?
A new price list with the reasoning behind each tier, the value metric it charges on, the migration path for existing customers, and the numbers the change is expected to move. A decision, not a deck.
Can you price a product that is still early?
Yes, and it is often the cheapest moment. Early pricing sets the value metric the product will be built around — much harder to change later than the number itself.
Do you take equity?
Advisory work is billed at 175 PLN per hour, or as a monthly retainer or fixed-scope engagement. Equity is only on the table for products I co-found.

What I don't do

  • No brand workshops and no forty-slide decks. If it cannot be decided in a document, it is not ready.
  • No roadmap built from customer-request tickets. Requests are evidence, not priorities.
  • No retainer without a decision to make.

Need a product brain for the next quarter?

Tell me what you sell, to whom, and what it costs. A thirty-minute call, then I come back with what I would change first, in writing.
